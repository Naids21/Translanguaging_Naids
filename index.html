      You ask for an explanation in English.

      Silence.

      02 / DISCOVER · ~1.25 MIN

      What is translanguaging?

      The strategic use of a learner’s full linguistic repertoire to make meaning, clarify concepts, communicate, and learn.

      KEY WORDStrategic.

      Not “anything goes.” Language is used intentionally to move learning forward.

      03 / DISTINGUISH · ~1.25 MIN

      Translanguaging is not simply code-switching.

      CODE-SWITCHING

      Language A ⇄ Language B

      Describes alternation between identifiable languages or language varieties.

      “I already submitted it, pero hindi pa na-check.”
      TRANSLANGUAGING

      Resources → Meaning → Learning

      Focuses on how learners draw across linguistic resources to accomplish a learning purpose.

      Read in English → discuss the idea → bridge key terms → produce the final English response.

      The difference is not only which language is used—it is what language is helping the learner accomplish.

      04 / CONNECT · ~1.25 MIN

      A recent shift in the language-policy landscape.

      2024

      Republic Act No. 12027

      Ends the mandatory use of mother tongue as the medium of instruction in K–3. Filipino and English are the primary media of instruction, while regional languages may still serve as auxiliary media.

      View law ↗
      2025

      DepEd Order No. 020, s. 2025

      For K–3, DepEd identifies translanguaging, scaffolding, and bridging among the linguistic supports schools may use.

      View order ↗
      Scope check: DepEd Order 020 is specifically a K–3 medium-of-instruction policy.

      06 / ILLUMINATE · ~1.5 MIN

      The destination can stay the same.
      The pathway can change.

      Target: Explain an argumentative claim in academic English.

      01THINKProcess the idea.
      02DISCUSSTest the meaning.
      03BRIDGEFind academic English.
      04PRODUCEDeliver the required English output.

      Translanguaging can change the route without changing the learning target.

      07 / WHY IT HELPS · ~1.5 MIN

      Three reasons it can help learning.

      ✦ 01

      Comprehension

      Unlock difficult concepts before limited target-language proficiency becomes a barrier.

      ✦ 02

      Participation

      Let learners contribute ideas before they can express those ideas in polished academic English.

      ✦ 03

      Bridging

      Move from familiar language toward disciplinary vocabulary and the required academic output.

      The goal is not “more languages.” The goal is better learning through language.

      08 / APPLY · ~1.25 MIN

      01

      Think → Bridge → Share

      Think: Process the idea using the language that helps you understand it.

      Bridge: Identify the English words and structures needed.

      Share: Express the response in the target language.

      “Ano ang point mo? Now, how can we express that idea academically in English?”

      09 / APPLY · ~1.5 MIN

      Two more classroom-ready moves.

      02

      Discuss → Refine → Produce

      Allow multilingual discussion of a difficult concept. Then help learners refine vocabulary and structure for the required output.

      The discussion may be multilingual; the assessed paragraph can still be in English.
      03

      Multilingual Annotation

      Allow notes in English, Filipino, a home language, symbols, or diagrams—while keeping key academic terms visible.

      counterclaim → salungat na argumento / kabilang panig
      THE PRINCIPLEUse language intentionally to move learners toward the learning objective.
